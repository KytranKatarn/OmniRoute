What Parliament Costs Canadians

The approximate cost to taxpayers of Canada's federal legislature — every Member of Parliament and senator's salary plus their office expenses (travel, hospitality, contracts and staff). Figures are drawn from public disclosures and the Library of Parliament's published salary tables. Pensions and some central costs are excluded. All figures are approximate and cited below.

What each Senate caucus’s senators cost — expenses by year

Senators’ expenses only (office, living, hospitality, travel and contracts) — salaries are not included here. Each quarter is charged to the caucus the senator belonged to in that quarter, retired senators included. Complete calendar years only; the Senate publishes these summaries from the third quarter of 2016, so 2017 is the first full year. The Senate’s 2016–17 transition labels “Non-affiliated (Independent Senators Group)” and “(Independent Reform)” are counted under Non-affiliated.

Earlier years — what each party’s MPs cost, by fiscal year

From the House’s annual per-member expense reports, before the quarterly disclosure began. These are fiscal years (April to March) — FY2010 is April 2009 to March 2010 — so they do not line up with the calendar years above and are not added to them. Each is the member’s office budget plus what the House provided (travel, phones, printing, supplies); salaries are not included. Each member’s whole year is charged to the party they sat for at the middle of the year: {{ annual_costs.changed }} member-years involved a mid-year party change and are counted wholly under one party. Vacant seats are not members and are left out.
