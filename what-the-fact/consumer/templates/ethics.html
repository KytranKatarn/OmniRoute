What a contravention finding is, and is not

A contravention means a specific section of a conflict-of-interest act or members’ code was breached. It is not a criminal finding. The Commissioner can investigate, find and publish. They cannot fine, sanction or remove anyone — at most they recommend a sanction to Parliament, which may ignore it.

The most common outcome of an ethics investigation is no contravention, and that is a real result rather than a technicality. Those reports are far less covered than adverse findings, which is a coverage bias this page cannot correct but can name.

Every row below names its subject, dates the ruling and links the report. A page that lists findings without a name, a date and a citation is unusable for a reader and indefensible for a site built on checkable claims.

About ethics investigations
The Commissioner investigates MPs and ministers under the Conflict of Interest Act (cabinet ministers, senior public servants) and the Conflict of Interest Code for Members (all 338 MPs). "Act" investigations are called examinations; "Code" investigations are called inquiries. Reports are public and link directly to CIEC findings.
