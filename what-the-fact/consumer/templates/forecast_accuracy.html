A.U.R.O.R.A. — forecast accountability

We keep score on the weather forecast, the same way we keep score on the news

Every daily high Environment Canada publishes for our 25 tracked cities is stored with the moment it was issued, then checked against the highest temperature the station actually recorded. The forecasts are Environment Canada’s; the scoring and the measurements are ours.

How this is measured

Average miss is the mean absolute difference between the forecast high and the observed high — how far off, ignoring direction. Direction of error is the mean signed difference — whether the forecast leans cold or warm. Both are shown because they answer different questions: a large miss with no direction is noise, while a small miss with a steady direction is a correctable systematic error. A day we did not observe produces no comparison at all; it is never scored as a perfect forecast. The persistence baseline above comes from the same observation series as everything else on this page — it is not a claim from any forecaster, just the naive answer of "no change".

Our record begins {% if s.window_start %}{{ s.window_start.strftime('%-d %B %Y') }}{% endif %} and covers {{ s.window_days }} days. That is enough to see a bias, not enough to call it seasonal — the window is stated here rather than left for the reader to assume, and it deepens by a day every day.
