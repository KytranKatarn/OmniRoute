THE PROCESS · FROM SOURCE TO STORY

This is the current ingest path, based on the WTF worker code. It runs in a cycle with separate backlog workers; it is not a guaranteed, one-agent-after-another workflow for every story.

  1. Collect and prepare

    The ingest worker polls configured feeds (30-minute default), normalizes items, applies a rules-based framing score and deduplicates before saving new articles.

  2. Group related coverage

    A title-token Jaccard similarity check groups articles within a 24-hour window. This is algorithmic clustering; the ingest path does not call a separate I.N.K. agent for each match.

  3. Select deeper analysis

    F.A.C.T.'s priority selector chooses some new articles. For selected items, the current cycle requests B.I.A.S. framing, V.E.R.I.F.Y. claims, beat classification and L.O.C.A.L. geography; backlog workers cover eligible older or pending items separately.

  4. Summarize and record

    After a cluster is updated, a summary attempt runs. Analysis and summary results are stored when available; failures and unselected items can leave fields empty or pending.

  5. Publish what is available

    Article and source pages show recorded signals and links to reporting. A page may have only a subset of the workflow results.

Selection, backlog configuration, queue capacity and service availability affect coverage. A missing signal does not mean “neutral,” “verified,” or “no issue.” Automated analysis can be wrong; follow the linked reporting and primary sources.

BIAS SCORING METHODOLOGY

Signals used when available

Bias analysis can draw on multiple signals. Which signals ran depends on the article and current system availability; additional signals do not make a score a fact or a quality rating.

  • Rules-based signal: deterministic language checks can contribute framing evidence from an article’s text.
  • Language-model signal: B.I.A.S. can return a structured framing score with rationale and trigger phrases for prioritized articles when that pass is available. It is an interpretation of language, not a truth verdict.
  • Additional model signals: independent automated signals may contribute when enabled and available. Model assignments and coverage can change; the current roster and its documented limits are on the team page.
  • Combined results: the displayed band follows the current scoring implementation and only uses signals available for that item. See the article page for which analysis was recorded.

FACT-CHECK METHODOLOGY

V.E.R.I.F.Y. Claim Extraction

V.E.R.I.F.Y. analyzes selected articles and extracts verifiable factual claims. Each claim can receive a verdict:

  • verified — the automated assessment judged the claim supported by available context; this is not independent proof
  • mixed — the assessment found partial support or missing context
  • disputed — the assessment found a conflict with available context
  • unverifiable — the assessment could not verify the specific claim from its available context
  • descriptive_reporting — no specific factual assertion was identified. This is not a quality judgment.

Automated verdicts can be wrong or out of date and should not be treated as independent proof. Follow the cited reporting and primary sources; the article page indicates which signals are available.

GEOLOCATION METHODOLOGY

L.O.C.A.L. Geo Extraction

L.O.C.A.L. adds geographic context using automated extraction with a deterministic fallback when confidence is low. The page’s live geolocation count shows the recorded coverage; a blank location is not inferred as “no location.”

KNOWN LIMITATIONS

Each limitation links to its entry in the independent C.R.E.E.D. Remediation Registry, which records our governance position and what we are doing about it.

  • AI analysis can be mistaken or out of date; consult the linked reporting and primary sources before relying on a result.Remediation ↗
  • Bias scoring reflects the LLM's interpretation of language patterns; it may not match human expert consensus in all cases.Remediation ↗
  • Geolocation uses source province as fallback when AI confidence is low (<0.5).Remediation ↗
  • Not every article receives every analysis pass. Coverage varies by queue and available signals; a missing score is not a neutral or verified result.Remediation ↗
  • Outlet bias profiles are rolling averages; new outlets are seeded from editorial stance data and refined over time.Remediation ↗
  • Some publishers expose only limited article text; analysis quality and available signals can be affected by that missing context.Remediation ↗
  • Scoring methods may not map perfectly to Canadian political framing or expert consensus; treat scores as estimates, not ground truth.Remediation ↗
