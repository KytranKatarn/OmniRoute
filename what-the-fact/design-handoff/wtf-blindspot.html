What one side is not seeing

Blindspot

Stories covered heavily in one part of the spectrum and barely at all in another. A blindspot is a coverage gap, not proof that a story is being suppressed — sometimes an outlet simply has no reporter on that beat.

A gap a few hours old is a news cycle. One that has stood while the story was widely available elsewhere is a choice, or a missing beat.

Counts are distinct outlets that published on the story within the window. A story needs at least four outlets in total before it can be called a blindspot, because two outlets ignoring something is not a pattern. Gap score is the difference between the two sides as a share of all coverage — |left − right| ÷ total × 100. The call itself uses a ratio, because a large centre pile dilutes that share: a side is named only when the quieter side holds a quarter or less of the two-sided coverage and is at least three pieces behind. Stories where neither side has four pieces are marked Sides too thin rather than balanced — an absence nobody contested is not the same as agreement.
