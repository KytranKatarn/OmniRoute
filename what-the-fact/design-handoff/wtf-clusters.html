I.N.K. · one event, many tellings

Story clusters

I.N.K. groups articles describing the same event so the spread of framing is visible in one place. A cluster is built from entities, dates and quoted figures, not from headline similarity — two stories that share a name are not the same story.

Sort by
How a cluster is built

I.N.K. matches articles on the entities they name, the dates they cite and the figures they quote. Two articles join a cluster when they share at least two of the three within a 72-hour window. Headline similarity is deliberately not a signal, because outlets rewrite headlines to differentiate, and the rewrite is often the framing worth measuring.

The median is the middle article’s band, not an average — a cluster of five Left and five Right has no meaningful average, and its median tells you which side has one more voice. The spread is the same 0–100 divergence score used on the Divergence page. A cluster under six outlets is flagged as a blindspot seed: it may be a small story, or a story only one part of the press has noticed.
