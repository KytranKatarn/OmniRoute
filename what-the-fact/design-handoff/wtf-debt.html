Debt tracker

Taxes in, spending out, and what the gap has added to the federal debt — ten fiscal years, so a reader can see whether Canada’s position is getting better or worse rather than being told. Every figure approximate, from the Public Accounts.

All amounts are Canadian dollars; B means billions, T trillions.

Ten fiscal years · CAD billions unless marked

Public Accounts of Canada and the Fiscal Monitor (Department of Finance). 2024–25 is the latest reported year and is flagged where preliminary. Series shapes are the design’s reading of published tables; the live page reads Finance Canada open data.

Revenue in, expenses out · every year on record here

The balance is revenue minus expenses; a negative number is a deficit and adds to the accumulated deficit, which is what “the federal debt” means in these tables. Provincial debt is not included.

Where it goes · 2024–25 expenses

Public debt charges — the interest on what has already been borrowed — now exceed what Ottawa transfers for health, and are the fastest-growing line. That comparison is the single most useful sentence on this page.

Where the money goes · by department and portfolio, 2024–25

Every federal dollar is spent through a department, agency or Crown corporation. Most of the biggest lines are money that passes straight through — pensions and benefits through Employment and Social Development, health and equalization transfers and the interest bill through Finance — not programs a department runs itself.

Public Accounts of Canada, Volume II (expenses by ministry), approximate and rounded; a portfolio groups a department with its agencies. Global Affairs is shown in the same scale so what Canada sends abroad can be read against what it spends at home.
