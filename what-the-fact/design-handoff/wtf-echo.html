Media · repetition, measured

Echo

How often a slogan, a phrase or a name repeats across Canadian coverage — when it was first heard, when it peaked, who keeps saying it, and which outlets repeat it verbatim. Counts of usage, never of merit: a phrase that echoes is not a phrase that is true.

Slogans in circulation · last 90 days

A slogan is counted when the exact phrase appears in an article or a filed statement. Pickup is the share of uses that came from outlets rather than from the speaker — the closest available measure of a phrase working. The lifecycle bars are weekly counts, first heard to now.

Words the news cannot stop using · mentions per day, 30 days

Across all 46 outlets in the feed. A mention is a mention: a story about a tariff and a story against one both count once.

Who posts most · public statements per week, by channel

Posts on the official accounts of party leaders and premiers, by platform, as captured by the ingest. Volume is a fact about output, not reach; reach lands when the social layer links in.

Fed by the Watch series · and, later, our own social layer

Today the counts come from the news corpus and filed statements. When the platform’s social monitoring links in, the same table gains a column for how each slogan performed in replies and shares — the difference between a phrase that is said and a phrase that lands.

What this page will not say

It will not say a slogan is effective, honest or good. It reports how often it was said and by whom. A phrase repeated by forty outlets may have been repeated because it was quotable, because it was false and needed rebutting, or because nothing else happened that day — and this page cannot tell those apart. The pickup column narrows the question; it does not answer it.
