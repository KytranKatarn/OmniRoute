Public money · abroad

Global Affairs spending

What Canada sends abroad from tax dollars — official development assistance, humanitarian aid, multilateral contributions and Ukraine support — who receives it, how it has moved, and how it sits against everything else the federal government spends. Every figure approximate and sourced.

All amounts are Canadian dollars. B in a chart or table means billions — $10.3B is ten billion, three hundred million dollars. The departmental picture this sits in is on the Debt tracker.

Ten years of international assistance · CAD, fiscal years

Source series: Global Affairs Canada, Statistical Report on International Assistance; OECD DAC ODA tables (CAD conversion at annual average). The series shape is the design’s reading of published totals; the live page reads GAC open data.

Where it goes · largest recipients and channels, latest reported year

Bilateral country programs are roughly a third; the rest flows through multilateral channels (World Bank IDA, UN agencies, the Global Fund) where a Canadian dollar is pooled with others’ and cannot be traced to a project.

In proportion · where aid sits in federal spending

International assistance is under two cents of every federal dollar. Coverage volume runs far ahead of that share — which is a fact about coverage, and the reason this page exists.

Cross-referenced with the Watch series

A recipient organization that also lobbies, wins federal contracts or is named in a ruling appears in Cross-Watch with every register it sits in. Appearing in a register is a record, not a finding.

Reading this honestly

“Sent abroad” includes money spent in Canada on resettling refugees, which the OECD counts as assistance; the tables say when it does. Loans to Ukraine are repayable and are shown separately from grants. Fiscal years end March 31, so a calendar-year story and a fiscal-year figure will not match, and neither is wrong.
