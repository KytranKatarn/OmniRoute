Bias scoring

B.I.A.S. scores an article, never an outlet. It compares one account of an event against every other account of the same event in the cluster, and places it on a five-band scale from Left to Right using the Canadian convention where left is red. A band is a reading of framing on that story: which facts led, which were omitted, whose words were quoted directly. The same masthead routinely lands in different bands in the same week, and that is the expected behaviour rather than a defect.

Fact-check verdicts

V.E.R.I.F.Y. only opens a claim it can settle against a primary document. Established means the document confirms the claim as stated. Contested means the document does not settle it — most often because the speaker and the source measure different things. Developing means the record is incomplete at time of checking. Nothing is marked false without the document that makes it false being cited alongside.

Missing data

An absence is drawn as an absence. A province with no reporting station is grey, not zero. A city with no observation says so rather than showing the last reading it had. A layer with no forward product is empty ahead of now instead of extrapolating one. This costs the site a lot of filled-in space, and it is the single rule we are least willing to trade away.

What we do not do

We do not score outlets permanently, rank them for trustworthiness, or average a bimodal cluster into a centre nobody published. We do not present a model prediction next to a measurement without saying which is which.
