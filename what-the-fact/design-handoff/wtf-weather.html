Fire spread prediction

Fire weather, sustained wind, temperature and current hotspot load, combined into a single 48-hour spread pressure for each province. This is decision support for reading the map, not a dispatch tool — it does not know where crews, water or roads are, and it should never be the reason anyone moves or stays.

Spread pressure by province, next 48 hours
Fire weather (FWI) Wind Temperature Current hotspot load
How the weight is set
Fire weather carries 40%, wind 30%, temperature 15%, existing hotspot load 15%. Wind is weighted heavily because it is what turns a contained fire into an uncontained one; hotspot load is weighted lightly because a detection is a signal, not a fire front.
What it cannot see
Fuel type and moisture, terrain and slope, crew and aircraft positions, existing fire breaks, and any province with no reporting station. In the territories the model is running on three stations, and it says so rather than smoothing over it.
Action plan — the next 24 hours

Where the model would put water down first, and the window in which the wind gives you a chance of it holding. Ordered by spread pressure, not by size of fire.

Read this before the plan above
This is a reading of wind, fire weather and temperature. It has no knowledge of aircraft availability, water sources, terrain, fuel moisture, crew safety or existing containment lines, and provincial fire centres have all of those. Nothing here is an operational instruction, and no one should move, stay or deploy on the strength of it. Where the model is running on fewer than four inputs it says so on the card.
