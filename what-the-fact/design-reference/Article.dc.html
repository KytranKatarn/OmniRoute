Canada’s economy can withstand an escalated trade war with U.S., say banks — but certain sectors will get hit hard

Bank economists say diversified exports and a weaker dollar give Canada room to absorb new tariffs — while steel, aluminum and auto parts brace for the worst of it.

Canada’s largest banks say the economy can absorb an escalated trade war with the United States, pointing to record export diversification and a currency that has already priced in much of the risk. Economists at three of the Big Five banks released assessments this week arguing the country’s position is stronger than in the 2018 tariff round.

The assessments land as Ottawa weighs its response to the latest round of U.S. measures. The banks caution that their outlook assumes retaliation stays targeted — a broad counter-tariff package would change the arithmetic quickly.

“The economy as a whole bends, but does not break. The pain is concentrated — and that concentration is the policy problem.”
SENIOR ECONOMIST, QUOTED IN THE ORIGINAL REPORTING

Steel, aluminum and auto parts remain the exposed flank. The sector-level analysis suggests output losses concentrated in southwestern Ontario and Quebec’s aluminum belt, with knock-on effects for regional employment that could persist beyond the tariff window itself.

The banks’ view contrasts with more pessimistic readings from industry associations, which argue the models understate supply-chain integration. Both readings agree on one thing: the longer the escalation lasts, the less the diversification story helps.
