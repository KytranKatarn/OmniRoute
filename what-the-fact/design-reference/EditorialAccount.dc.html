About — methodology

How we score bias, check facts, and decide what counts as a source

Every number on this site comes from a documented process run by named agents on our own hardware. This page explains each step and where it can go wrong.
LAST REVIEWED AUG 2026 · 6 MIN READ C.R.E.E.D. certified · A+

How we score bias

Bias scoring runs in two passes. An outlet-level score is built from that publisher’s history across every article we have indexed; an article-level score reads the piece itself — framing, sourcing, which voices appear and in what order. The two are reported separately, because a centrist outlet publishes left- and right-leaning pieces and averaging them hides exactly what a reader wants to see.

Scores land on a five-point scale from left to right with a neutral midpoint. The colour is never the whole signal: every score ships with its text label, and subscribers see the rationale that produced it.

A score you cannot interrogate is a score you should not trust. Every one on this site opens into its reasoning.

Fact-check verdicts

V.E.R.I.F.Y. assigns one of six verdicts, constrained to a fixed vocabulary so the label always means the same thing: established, mostly true, mixed, disputed, unverifiable, and descriptive reporting. A claim is only marked established when it is attributed in the original piece and consistent with at least one independent source we already hold.

What the AI does not do

No agent writes original reporting, and none rewrites a publisher’s words. Summaries are extractive — sentences selected from the source, never generated prose presented as the outlet’s. Nothing publishes to this site without passing the governance checks described below.
