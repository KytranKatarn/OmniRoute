What we found The long-range forecast runs cold
Five and six days out, the published high comes in 1.2°C below what the day actually reached — consistently, not randomly. Same-day forecasts show almost no bias at all (−0.09°).
A consistent bias is a correctable one. That is what A.U.R.O.R.A. does next.

How this is measured
Each published daily high is stored with the moment it was issued. When that day ends, we take the highest temperature the station actually recorded and compare. Average miss is the mean absolute difference — how far off, ignoring direction. Direction of error is the mean signed difference — whether it leans cold or warm.
Our record begins 18 August 2026. Twelve days is enough to see a bias, not enough to call it seasonal. The window is stated on every figure and deepens daily.
