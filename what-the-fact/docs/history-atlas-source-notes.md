# Canadian Historical Atlas — 159 draft events

Adds 23 source-linked records: Filles du Roi, census history, colonial towns, municipal incorporation, northern capitals and the Northwest Territories transfer. Stable event identifiers and existing records are retained.

This is a cumulative research base, not a complete history or publication-ready database. Each event carries sources, dating precision and map limitations. Some sources were inspected as search extracts rather than full pages. Indigenous community histories require nation-authored research; incorporation dates do not date human occupation. Empty peoples arrays mean further community-specific sourcing is needed, not an absence of Indigenous peoples.

Use province_context only for present-day navigation. It is not the jurisdiction at the event date. The Arctic map filter currently groups northern territories; it does not mean Whitehorse is geographically in the Arctic.

Map layers: modern coast reference, undated Native Land reference territories, and NRCan political snapshots from 1867. No exact municipal boundaries, voyage tracks or pre-1867 colonial boundaries are asserted by these additions. Resolve Native Land licensing before commercial deployment.

## Coverage plan for every province and territory

Research Indigenous nations and place names; archaeology with date ranges; colonial governance; Confederation or territorial creation; boundary changes; capital history; city incorporation and annexations; treaties and land agreements; migration; infrastructure and industry; rights and exclusions; cultural and environmental history.

Federation milestones already represented: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick (1867); Manitoba and Northwest Territories (1870); British Columbia (1871); Prince Edward Island (1873); Yukon (1898); Alberta and Saskatchewan (1905); Newfoundland (1949); Nunavut (1999). Earlier and later names and boundaries require separate records.

Remaining city coverage priorities: St. John’s, Charlottetown, Fredericton, Québec municipal history, Trois-Rivières, Saskatoon, northern regional centres, Indigenous urban histories, annexations and amalgamations. Remaining national priorities include the 1759–1763 conquest sequence, rebellions, public health, social programmes, language legislation, modern treaties and constitutional debates. These are research priorities, not verified new entries.

## Migration expansion

Nine additional history entries and thirteen selected movement records. Filters distinguish settlement/family movement, labour, refuge/escape, child emigration, forced movement and seasonal travel. Movement windows are not continuous annual flow measurements. Home Children ends in the approximate late 1930s, encoded with 1939 as an upper bound; Underground Railroad start is a rounded early-century bound, not an institutional founding date.

Exact routes remain null. The map uses province/territory geometry centroids as broad regional orientation. Origin labels can lie outside the current North American map. A marker can represent an arrival gateway, an example camp or an origin, rather than a final destination. A Quebec-to-Nunavut connector represents only one branch of the Inuit relocation, not both origins or exact travel. Counts in map circles are numbers of records, never numbers of migrants.

Next priorities: Indigenous seasonal networks and inter-community movement using community sources; Abenaki displacement; Acadian refuge, return and Louisiana settlement as distinct legs; Métis mobility; Loyalist and planter migrations; Prairie settlement waves; departures to the United States; Jewish, Ukrainian, Italian and other migration histories; wartime dispossession and residential-school removals; urbanization and interprovincial work migration; temporary workers; later refugee programmes. No single set of arrows can stand in for these histories.

Other national gaps: slavery and abolition; conquest and rebellions; Canadian citizenship; the flag; official languages and Quebec language policy; the Quiet Revolution; constitutional referendums; health care and social programmes; industrialization and resource extraction; environmental change; women’s and minority rights; Indigenous political resurgence and modern agreements. Dates and claims require further source review before addition.

## Borders and map images

Adds 12 draft events. The 23 loaded NRCan snapshot years now support a preceding-snapshot comparison and rendered map image for each selected post-1867 period. These images are newly rendered from published geometry, not historic photographs. A prior-snapshot outline is an administrative reference, not a calculated area of territorial gain or loss. Label differences include renaming and subdivision; they do not by themselves identify territorial expansion. Within-year legal commencement dates remain unresolved.

Includes the published generalized topology so the source-derived maps can be reproduced. Indigenous reference territory geometry remains undated and carries unresolved licensing review before commercial use. No pre-1867 colonial geometry or legal treaty areas are added. The 1763 LOC map is a catalogue reference only: page access failed, image is not embedded, and rights are not yet verified.
